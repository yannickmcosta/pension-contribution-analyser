    New to this? Four steps.

    1. Open Settings and check three things: how often you are paid, the contribution percentages your scheme quotes, and what your scheme says those percentages are calculated on. All of that is in your pension enrolment letter.
    2. Add a payslip and copy across four figures from it: your gross pay, the pension amount deducted from your pay, and the employer contribution. Leave the pensionable pay box empty unless your pensionable pay differs from your gross pay.
    3. Repeat for each payslip you want to check. Use Duplicate Last when the figures are the same.
    4. Read the banner at the top. It states plainly whether your contributions match what your scheme says, and if not, by how much.

    Base / pensionable salary

    The salary or pay figure against which a particular pension scheme has stated contributions will be calculated. If your contract or scheme documentation says contributions are a percentage of your basic salary, then your basic salary is the pensionable pay figure and the whole of it counts.

    Qualifying earnings

    A statutory earnings band used for automatic enrolment minimum contribution calculations. Only the slice of earnings between a lower and an upper threshold counts. Earnings below the lower threshold are ignored entirely, and earnings above the upper threshold are capped. Because it is only a band, it produces much smaller contributions than a full-salary basis for anyone earning above the upper threshold.

    Qualifying earnings for a pay period are: max(0, min(gross pay, upper threshold) − lower threshold)

    Relief at Source (RAS)

    Under Relief at Source — the method NEST normally uses — you pay your contribution out of pay that has already been taxed, and the pension provider then claims basic-rate tax relief from HMRC and adds it to your pot. This is why the amount deducted from your payslip is smaller than the amount that actually lands in your pension.

    This tool therefore distinguishes carefully between:

    • Employee deduction from payslip — the net amount taken from your pay.
    • Gross employee pension contribution — the deduction plus the basic-rate tax relief reclaimed by the provider.

    For a 20% RAS scheme, £40 deducted from pay corresponds to a £50 gross pension contribution once £10 of basic-rate relief is added. Working backwards, the tool derives your gross contribution as deduction ÷ (1 − relief rate).

    The comparison, step by step

    1. Expected on base salary

    • Employer = pensionable pay × employer %
    • Employee gross = pensionable pay × employee %
    • Employee net deduction = employee gross × (1 − relief rate)
    • Tax relief = employee gross − employee net
    • Total = employer + employee gross

    2. Actual, from the payslip

    • Employee net deduction = as printed on the payslip
    • Employee gross = deduction ÷ (1 − relief rate)
    • Relief claimed = employee gross − deduction
    • Employer = as printed on the payslip
    • Total = employer + employee gross

    3. Expected on qualifying earnings

    • Qualifying earnings = banded gross pay
    • The same percentages and relief rate are then applied
    • Shown so you can see whether your payslip is following the statutory minimum band rather than your stated salary

    Differences

    Differences are calculated as expected on base salary minus actual. A positive figure is a shortfall (less went in than the base salary basis would give). A negative figure is labelled an overpayment rather than shown as a negative shortfall.

    Effective contribution rates

    The same difference expressed as a percentage: what the money actually paid works out at as a share of your base/pensionable pay, next to the rate your scheme states. A qualifying earnings calculation on a salary above the upper threshold produces a much lower effective rate than the headline one, because only the slice of pay inside the band attracts contributions. This is shown for the whole period on the summary cards and per payslip in the row detail, and it is a restatement of the same figures, not a separate calculation.

    Detected calculation basis

    The actual employer contribution and actual employee deduction are compared, within the configured tolerance, against both the base salary figures and the qualifying earnings figures. The result is shown as Matches Base Salary, Matches Qualifying Earnings, Does Not Match Either or Insufficient Data. A mismatch is not in itself evidence of an error — see the note at the foot of the page.

    Rounding

    Every money figure is rounded to whole pence, half away from zero, at each step of the calculation, which is the usual payroll convention. The comparison tolerance (default £0.02) absorbs the small differences that arise from different rounding points in payroll software.

    Important. This tool is intended to assist with checking pension contribution calculations. It does not provide legal, financial or tax advice. Pension scheme rules, earnings definitions and tax treatment can vary. Check your employment terms, pension scheme documentation, payroll records and current statutory guidance.

    A difference between the figures shown here and your payslip does not automatically mean anything has gone wrong. Employers and schemes may legitimately use a different definition of pensionable pay, a different earnings basis, salary sacrifice, a different tax relief method, or contribution caps. The purpose of this tool is to show you clearly which basis appears to have been used so that you can ask an informed question.

    Statutory qualifying earnings thresholds and tax rates change from time to time. All thresholds and percentages used here are editable in Settings and should be checked against current guidance before you rely on any figure.
